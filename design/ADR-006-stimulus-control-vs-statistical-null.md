# ADR-006: Stimulus control is random pairing; the statistical null is label permutation

**Status:** Accepted, 2026-09-13. Corrects an ambiguity in BRIEF.md.

## Context

The founding brief described two different things as "the null": a Wainrib-style label permutation (permute dream-to-note assignments; if the hit rate survives, there was no signal) and a weekend gate that emits "permuted or scrambled controls (same concepts, wrong pairings)" for blind scoring. The adversarial review pointed out these answer different questions, and that scrambled-pair controls can be trivially distinguishable for the wrong reason: they are incoherent, so the rater spots them by fluency, not insight. That is the CHI 2024 lesson turned against us.

## Decision

Two separate mechanisms, both preregistered:

1. Stimulus control (what the rater sees): arm B1, random pairing, generated by the identical pipeline and prompt. Both arms are fluent LLM prose; the only difference is the sampling policy. B4, single-note reflection with no recombination, is the incubation control.
2. Statistical null (how significance is computed): shuffle arm labels across all scored items 10,000 times, recompute the KEEP-rate gap, and report the permutation p alongside a one-sided Fisher exact test.

Matched-decoy pairs remain a secondary base-rate check in Track A.

## Consequences

- Rater blinding is over arms, not over real-vs-fake.
- "Signal" in v0.1 means permutation p < 0.05 for B3 or B6 against B1, and the winner above B4.
- Scoring happens pre-critic in v0.1 to keep power (critic kill rates near 85% would leave about 15 items per arm).
